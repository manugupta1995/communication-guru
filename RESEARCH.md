# 📚 Communication Guru — Research Foundation
### What makes a great speaker, and what we measure

**Author:** Manu Gupta · **Date:** June 2026 · **Companion to:** PRD.md

This document is the scientific backbone for the product. It answers two questions:
1. **What actually makes someone a great speaker?** (not folk wisdom — classical rhetoric + modern empirical research)
2. **What does the user get out of the app?** (measurable layers + a teachable path)

---

## 1. The core reframe

Most tools (Yoodli, Orai) measure **delivery only** — filler words, words-per-minute, pauses. But delivery is just **one of five canons** of rhetoric. A speaker can have flawless delivery and still fail because the *substance*, *structure*, or *language* was weak.

Great speaking is a **stack of five layers, situated inside a rhetorical context.** We measure the whole stack.

Two classical frameworks define it:

- **The Rhetorical Situation** = the *frame* (Topic, Setting, Audience, Occasion, Credibility). Aristotle: rhetoric is "the ability to identify the available means of persuasion **in a given situation**." You never judge a speech in a vacuum — context decides what "good" means.
- **The Five Canons of Rhetoric** (Cicero) = the *measurement layers*: **Invention, Arrangement, Style, Memory, Delivery.**

---

## 2. The Rhetorical Situation (the frame)

Five elements the speaker must read before a single word: **rhetor (credibility/ethos), audience, message, purpose (telos), context.** This maps directly to the "Topic / Setting / Audience / Occasion / Credibility" model.

**Aristotle's three appeals** sit inside this frame:
- **Ethos** — credibility/character of the speaker ("persuasion through character").
- **Pathos** — emotional connection with the audience's values.
- **Logos** — logic and structure of the argument itself.

**Product implication:** before recording, the user sets Audience + Occasion + Purpose. The scoring rubric **re-weights** accordingly (an exec update rewards concision + gravitas; a toast rewards pathos + story). Same engine, situational scoring.

*Sources:* Purdue OWL — Aristotle's Rhetorical Situation; Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Aristotle's Rhetoric; The Decision Lab — Ethos/Pathos/Logos.

## 3. The Great Speaker Stack — the five measurable layers

| # | Canon | What it really is | Measurable signals (how we score it) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Invention** | Substance: argument quality, evidence, the ethos/pathos/logos mix | claim→evidence ratio; specificity vs. vagueness; relevance to question/audience; presence of a clear point |
| 2 | **Arrangement** | Structure: hook, logical order, signposting, strong close | structure detection (STAR / problem-solution); has opening hook?; has clear close?; transition quality; "buried the lede" detection |
| 3 | **Style** | Language: clarity, concision, vividness, jargon | avg sentence length; concreteness; jargon/buzzword density; readability / processing-fluency score; one-idea-per-sentence |
| 4 | **Memory** | Command of material: fluency, recall, recovery, not reading | fluency under load; filler spikes at think-points; recovery after a stumble; over-scripted vs. natural |
| 5 | **Delivery** | Vocal + nonverbal (the Yoodli layer) | **vocal variety** (pace & pitch variance); pause use; filler rate; energy/volume dynamics; (video) gestures, eye contact, expression |

### Why each layer is real (empirical backing)

- **Delivery — vocal variety is the single biggest measured lever.** Science-of-People's study of 760 raters across TED talks found the most popular speakers had **+30.5% higher vocal variety** (deliberate variation in pace, emphasis, energy). Strikingly, raters scored charisma/credibility **the same whether audio was on or muted** — audiences judge nonverbal signals **before processing content.** *(TED Blog; Science of People.)*

- **Arrangement & Style — processing fluency.** Information that is easy to process is better understood *and* triggers a more positive affective response; disorganized info imposes cognitive load that is "inherently unpleasant." Clear structure literally makes you more persuasive. *(LSE Public Policy Review.)*

- **Invention/Style — engagement beats raw structure.** A computational study (ARGUS) found **reader-response features (curiosity, suspense) had larger persuasive effects than structural features** — i.e., *how you manage information disclosure* matters as much as the skeleton. Storytelling with relatable conflict/resolution changes beliefs more than facts alone. *(arXiv ARGUS; LSE PPR.)*

- **Memory & all layers — it's trainable.** Public-speaking confidence is "a trainable skill, not a fixed personality trait"; most people see improvement within a **few weeks of deliberate practice** when targeting specific areas with feedback. This validates the per-layer drill loop. *(Connected Speech Pathology; eCampusOntario.)*

## 4. The goal state: warmth × competence (teachable charisma)

Research on **executive presence** converges on three levers: **gravitas** (calm/credibility under pressure), **high-impact communication** (clarity + synthesis), and **credibility** (words match actions). Charisma = **warmth + competence**: authority balanced with approachability.

Critically for a product: **charisma can be taught** — empirically — via storytelling, vocal variety, and strategic vulnerability. Executive presence reportedly accounts for **>25% of what determines a promotion** (Hewlett). This is the north-star metric the app moves users toward.

*Sources:* Brown Professional Studies — ABCs of Executive Presence; IMD — Mastering Executive Presence; Science of People — teaching charisma.

---

## 5. What the user actually gets (the answer to "what's the value?")

Not "you said *um* 12 times." Instead, per session:

1. **A scored profile across all 5 layers** — so they see their weakest layer, which is usually NOT delivery.
2. **The #1 weak layer named**, with a **technique from a real rhetorical tradition** (e.g., Arrangement → "signpost-and-close"; Memory → "pause-and-breathe"; Delivery → "vocal-variety drill").
3. **A 30–60s micro-drill** for that technique.
4. **A re-record** that proves the layer score moved.
5. **Week-over-week tracking** toward the warmth×competence target — the measurable improvement competitors don't show.
